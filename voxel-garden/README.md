# Tenkū no Niwa 天空の庭 — a voxel garden in the sky

A Japanese garden built from voxels and floating above a sea of clouds: a five-storey pagoda, a
tea house, a koi pond with an arched bridge, a raked rock garden, a bamboo grove, a shrine with a
row of small torii, tiny villagers going about their day, and a dragon circling over it all.
One `index.html`, no assets, no build step. Three.js loads from a CDN; every block, person, fish,
dragon scale and sound is generated by code.

**▶ [Open it](https://badboyvee.github.io/BlackHoleeee/voxel-garden/)** — or download
[`index.html`](index.html) and open it in any modern browser.

## What the villagers do

Thirteen of them follow one network of garden paths. A monk sweeps the pagoda terrace; the tea
keeper kneels at her table and now and then carries a tray to the guest on the veranda; an old man
fishes at the pond (and sometimes gets a bite); a gardener prunes and waters; a pilgrim bows at the
torii, prays at the shrine and rings the bell; children run about; strollers feed the koi from the
bridge, sit on benches, meditate beside the rock garden, and point at the dragon when it passes
overhead. Each routine is a small generator function (`yield walk('bell1')`, `yield pose('pray', 3)`).

## How it's made

- **Voxel engine.** The island is one `224 × 144 × 224` grid, cut into 32³ chunks and meshed with
  hidden faces removed and per-vertex ambient occlusion baked in. A quad is split along its brighter
  diagonal so occlusion reads as corner blobs instead of streaks. A few small voxel models (the
  villagers, lanterns, koi, dragon segments) are meshed the same way but live as separate objects.
- **One patched material.** Everything is a three.js `MeshLambertMaterial` with an `onBeforeCompile`
  patch. Per-voxel *tags* tell the GPU what a block is: leaves and grass are recoloured by season,
  sway in the wind and shrink away when they fall; exposed tops turn to snow; windows glow by time of
  day; and 28 lantern lights are evaluated in the fragment shader, so the whole garden can have
  dozens of warm point lights without the cost of real ones. A matching depth material keeps shadows
  honest when leaves vanish in winter.
- **Roofs** come from one hip-roof function with a concave profile and sweeping corners; the pagoda,
  tea house, bell tower, shrine and even the stone lanterns reuse it.
- **The dragon** is a closed Catmull-Rom flight path sampled into frames. Forty-four body slices (eight
  tapering sizes, instanced) follow the head with a travelling wave, a roll for the barrel roll and a
  bank into turns; whiskers are a little chain simulation.
- **Sound** is all WebAudio: a temple bell built from inharmonic partials, pentatonic pagoda chimes,
  a shishi-odoshi clack, a layered dragon roar, wind, water, birdsong, crickets and a sparse generative
  koto, with a generated reverb. Nothing is downloaded.
- **Post.** Half-float render target with MSAA, bloom for lanterns and fireflies, and a gentle
  tilt-shift and vignette for the miniature look. A ⚙ button cycles Low / Medium / High, and the
  garden steps itself down if the frame rate drops.

## Notes if you want to hack on it

Everything is in `index.html`: the interface at the top, then one ES module. Sections are marked with
banner comments: core · voxel engine · sky & light · terrain · structures · plants · water · props ·
villagers · dragon · wildlife & fx · audio · interaction & UI · main loop.

- Layout lives in the `LAY` object (garden coordinates: `u` east, `v` south, origin at the island
  centre); the paths in `NODES` / `ROUTES` are used both to paint the gravel and to steer villagers.
- Palette entries are added with `def(name, hex)`; tagged entries (`T.SAKURA`, `T.EM_PAGODA`...) get
  seasonal or glowing behaviour. The grid stores 16-bit palette ids.
- Add `?q=0|1|2` to pick the graphics tier, `?season=0..3`, `?hour=17.5`, `?still` to stop the clock,
  or `?nointro` to skip the opening camera glide.
- Three.js is pinned to `0.186.0` in the import map at the top of the file.
